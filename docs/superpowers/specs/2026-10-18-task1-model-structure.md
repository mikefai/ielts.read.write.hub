# Task 1 model structure (reference for the six Task 1 notes)

Task 1: an objective report of at least 150 words (about 20 minutes) on visual data. 3-4 paragraphs: Introduction (paraphrase, 1 sentence), Overview (2 sentences, crucial for Band 7+), Body 1 (3-4 sentences), Body 2 (3-4 sentences). No personal opinion, speculation or outside reasons. Source: the teacher's model, 2026-10-18. Spelling converted to British.

Note-to-model mapping:
- `task1-chart` = Dynamic (line graph over time), model A.
- `task1-table` = Static comparison, model B.
- `task1-pie` = Static comparison; NO model given: follow the model B pattern (intro 1, overview 2, body 1 = largest slices 3 sentences, body 2 = smaller slices/changes 3 sentences) with 9 sentences using only the numbers already in the note's current demo (keep every figure consistent, percentages must not exceed 100 in total).
- `task1-mixed` = Mixed charts, model C.
- `task1-process` = Process, model D (10 sentences).
- `task1-map` = Map, model E.

## A. Dynamic (line graph) — note `task1-chart`
Prompt: The line graph shows changes in the consumption of three types of fast food in the United Kingdom between 1970 and 1990.
Grammar focus: past tenses, trend verbs (increased, fell steadily). Overview must give general directions + the extreme endpoint or cross-over. Body 1 = rising trends; body 2 = declining trends.
1. Intro: The line graph illustrates changes in the consumption levels of three different types of fast food in the United Kingdom between 1970 and 1990.
2. Overview, overall trend: Overall, it is clear that while the consumption of hamburgers and pizza experienced a marked upward trajectory throughout the period, fish and chips followed a significant downward trend.
3. Overview, highest/lowest or intersection: Additionally, despite starting as by far the least favoured item, hamburgers overtook fish and chips to become the most consumed food by the final year.
4. Body 1, starting point: In 1970, hamburger intake stood at approximately 80 grams per person per week.
5. Body 1, growth and milestone: This figure climbed steadily over the next decade before accelerating rapidly after 1980 to reach a peak of 500 grams in 1990.
6. Body 1, second rising category: Similarly, pizza consumption began at roughly 20 grams, experiencing a consistent, fourfold expansion to finish at nearly 280 grams.
7. Body 2, starting figure of declining category: In stark contrast, fish and chips was initially the most popular option, with weekly consumption hovering around 300 grams in 1970.
8. Body 2, decline and cross-over: Although this number fluctuated slightly during the 1970s, it plummeted sharply after 1980, dipping below hamburgers in 1985.
9. Body 2, ending value: By 1990, fish and chips intake had dropped to a terminal low of just under 150 grams.

## B. Static (bar chart) — note `task1-table`
Prompt: The bar chart compares the proportion of household expenditure on five categories in four European countries in 2020.
Grammar focus: comparatives and superlatives (far higher than, outnumbered by). Overview must give the highest and lowest categories + a country pattern. Body 1 = highest-ranking categories; body 2 = lower-ranking or minor categories.
1. Intro: The bar chart compares the proportion of household expenditure allocated to five distinct categories across four European nations in 2020.
2. Overview, most/least significant: Overall, housing and food accounted for the vast majority of consumer spending across all surveyed nations, whereas leisure and clothing consistently represented the smallest budgetary shares.
3. Overview, country highlight: Furthermore, French households dedicated the largest share of their expenditure to food, while the United Kingdom recorded the highest proportion spent on housing.
4. Body 1, leading country in category 1: Looking first at housing, the UK led all countries with 32% of total domestic spending directed toward accommodation.
5. Body 1, comparison in category 1: This was closely followed by France and Germany at 28% and 26% respectively, while Spain registered the lowest allocation at just 18%.
6. Body 1, contrast with food: Regarding food, Spain and France allocated the largest percentages (30% and 27%), whereas British consumers spent significantly less on groceries, at only 15%.
7. Body 2, low-spending categories: In terms of lower-cost sectors, spending on clothing and recreation rarely exceeded 10% in any nation.
8. Body 2, exact figures: German households spent marginally more on entertainment (9%) than their Spanish counterparts (5%), while clothing expenditure remained remarkably uniform across all four countries at roughly 6% to 7%.
9. Body 2, smallest metric: The smallest single recorded metric was Spanish recreation spending, which bottomed out at just 4%.

## C. Mixed charts (pie + table) — note `task1-mixed`
Prompt: The pie chart shows the main reasons adults travelled abroad in 2018, and the table shows average holiday spending across four age groups in the same year.
Grammar focus: referential linkers (Turning to..., Regarding...). Overview = one top feature of each chart. Body 1 = full breakdown of chart 1; body 2 = full breakdown of chart 2. There is NO extra linking sentence in this model.
1. Intro (paraphrase both graphics): The pie chart displays the primary motivations for international travel among adults in 2018, while the accompanying table outlines average holiday expenditures across four distinct age demographics in the same year.
2. Overview, chart 1: Overall, tourism was overwhelmingly the most prevalent reason for travelling abroad, accounting for over half of all departures.
3. Overview, chart 2: Simultaneously, holiday spending was directly proportional to age, with older travellers expending substantially more funds than younger cohorts.
4. Body 1, dominant segment: According to the pie chart, holidaymaking was the primary purpose for 55% of all foreign trips.
5. Body 1, secondary segments: Visiting friends and relatives formed the second largest motivation at 23%, followed by business trips, which comprised 17%.
6. Body 1, residual segment: The remaining 5% was attributed to miscellaneous or unspecified travel reasons.
7. Body 2, highest-spending group: Turning to the table, individuals aged 60 and above incurred the highest average trip expenses, spending approximately $2,400 per person.
8. Body 2, middle tiers: Those in the 40-59 and 25-39 brackets recorded moderate spending averages of $1,800 and $1,250 respectively.
9. Body 2, lowest group and comparison: Conversely, younger travellers under 25 spent the least, averaging a mere $650 per journey, which was roughly one-quarter of the senior bracket's expenditure.

## D. Process — note `task1-process`
Prompt: The diagram shows the stages in the commercial production of olive oil.
Grammar focus: passive voice (is harvested, are transported) + sequence linkers. Overview = number of stages, start input and end output. Body 1 = first half of the stages; body 2 = second half.
1. Intro: The diagram illustrates the sequential stages involved in the commercial production of olive oil from raw fruit to the final packaged commodity.
2. Overview, scope and ends: Overall, the process consists of a seven-stage linear procedure, commencing with the harvesting of mature olives and culminating in the bottling and distribution of refined oil.
3. Overview, key transformation: The entire operation relies on mechanical separation, washing, and cold-pressing rather than chemical synthesis.
4. Body 1, harvesting and cleaning: In the initial stage, ripe olives are harvested from trees and transported to a processing facility where leaves and debris are mechanically sifted out.
5. Body 1, washing: Following this, the fruit is thoroughly rinsed with cold water on a specialised conveyor belt to eliminate surface contaminants.
6. Body 1, crushing: Once cleaned, the olives are fed into a mechanical mill where they are crushed into a thick, uniform paste.
7. Body 1, malaxation: This paste is subsequently stirred in a heated malaxing vat for 40 minutes to facilitate oil droplet coalescence.
8. Body 2, centrifuge: At the subsequent stage, the mixture undergoes high-speed centrifugal spinning to separate solid pomace from liquid oil and water.
9. Body 2, filtration: The extracted liquid is then filtered once more to remove lingering water residues, producing pure, extra-virgin olive oil.
10. Body 2, packaging: Finally, the purified oil is transferred into airtight glass containers, sealed, and dispatched to retail outlets for consumer purchase.

## E. Map — note `task1-map`
Prompt: The two maps show changes in the coastal town of Seabourne between 1995 and the present day.
Grammar focus: passive transformations (was demolished, was converted into) + prepositions of place and compass directions. Overview = degree of development + the main replacement/addition. Body 1 = changes in one zone (north/coast); body 2 = changes in the opposite zone (south/inland).
1. Intro: The two maps depict the architectural and infrastructural transformations that occurred in the coastal town of Seabourne between 1995 and the present day.
2. Overview, macro change: Overall, the town has undergone extensive modernisation and commercial expansion, transitioning from a quiet fishing village into an active tourist destination.
3. Overview, main additions and losses: The most prominent developments include the removal of maritime industrial sites and the extensive construction of recreational facilities along the waterfront.
4. Body 1, removal on the shoreline: In the northern sector, the original fish market and warehouse docks situated along the coast have been completely demolished.
5. Body 1, new facilities: In their place, a large luxury marina and an open-air promenade have been constructed to accommodate leisure vessels.
6. Body 1, adjacent infrastructure: Immediately to the east of the new marina, a previously barren stretch of beach has been converted into a beachfront hotel complex complete with a private swimming pool.
7. Body 2, residential changes: Moving inland toward the southern half of the town, significant changes have been made to residential accommodation and transit links.
8. Body 2, housing and tree removal: A grove of woodland situated in the south-west was cleared to make way for a block of residential apartments and a car park.
9. Body 2, roads: Furthermore, the main southern road was widened, and a newly built roundabout now connects the residential sector directly to the hotel district.
