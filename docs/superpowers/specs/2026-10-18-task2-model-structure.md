# Task 2 model structure (reference for the five essay notes)

Every Task 2 essay: Introduction (2 sentences), Body 1 (4 sentences), Body 2 (4 sentences), Conclusion (2 sentences) = 12 sentences. Body sentence functions: topic sentence, explanation, example (or concession/second factor), result/anchor. Source: the teacher's model, 2026-10-18. Spelling below is converted to British.

## opinion — id `opinion`
Prompt: Some believe university education should be free for all students. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
1. Intro, paraphrase: It is frequently argued that higher education should be funded entirely by the state rather than individual attendees.
2. Intro, clear thesis and stance: I completely agree with this view because universal tertiary schooling reduces socio-economic inequality and boosts overall national economic productivity.
3. Body 1, topic sentence: The primary rationale for subsidising university tuition is the promotion of social mobility.
4. Body 1, explanation: When high tuition fees are removed, students from underprivileged backgrounds can pursue professional careers without incurring crippling debt.
5. Body 1, example: For instance, in countries like Germany and Norway, where public universities charge minimal administrative fees, enrolment rates among first-generation academics are significantly higher.
6. Body 1, result: Consequently, state-funded higher education acts as an effective equaliser across income brackets.
7. Body 2, topic sentence: Furthermore, universal access to advanced education yields substantial macroeconomic benefits for the nation.
8. Body 2, explanation: A highly educated populace expands the skilled labour force, which fosters technological innovation and attracts foreign enterprise.
9. Body 2, concession: Although funding these programmes requires considerable fiscal expenditure, the long-term tax revenues generated by high-earning graduates comfortably offset initial public costs.
10. Body 2, result: Thus, subsidising tuition proves to be a profitable long-term investment rather than an economic liability.
11. Conclusion, restate thesis and main points: In conclusion, I firmly maintain that university tuition should be entirely free, as it promotes equitable social advancement and strengthens national competitiveness.
12. Conclusion, final takeaway: Governments worldwide ought to prioritise tertiary funding to foster resilient knowledge-based economies.

## discussion — id `discussion`
Prompt: Some people think that computers are more reliable than human workers, while others argue that human labour is superior. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
Order rule: Body 1 = the view you do NOT hold (explored fairly); Body 2 = the view you hold, with your stance.
1. Intro, paraphrase both views: While some individuals contend that automation and computing systems outperform human workers, others assert that human labour remains indispensable.
2. Intro, thesis and opinion: Although machines offer unparalleled operational precision, I believe that human emotional intelligence and creative adaptability remain fundamentally superior in the workforce.
3. Body 1, topic sentence: Proponents of automated systems primarily cite their superior efficiency and consistency.
4. Body 1, explanation: Unlike biological workers, algorithms and robotic systems can operate uninterrupted without suffering from fatigue, cognitive bias, or stress.
5. Body 1, example: In data-intensive fields like financial bookkeeping or high-volume manufacturing, computational processors execute complex operations with near-zero error margins.
6. Body 1, summary of view 1: For this reason, algorithmic labour is widely regarded as far more reliable in repetitive execution.
7. Body 2, turn to your view: Nevertheless, I hold that human professionals possess unique cognitive capacities that synthetic systems cannot replicate.
8. Body 2, explanation: Nuanced problem-solving, ethical judgement, and empathetic communication require human consciousness and contextual awareness.
9. Body 2, example: In medical diagnostics and senior management, critical decisions often hinge on ambiguous qualitative criteria where mechanical data interpretation fails.
10. Body 2, result: Therefore, while machines excel at computation, human labour remains the cornerstone of sophisticated and compassionate decision-making.
11. Conclusion, synthesise both sides and restate stance: In conclusion, although computerised automation undeniably maximises operational speed and output accuracy, I maintain that humans are irreplaceable due to their critical thinking and interpersonal intuition.
12. Conclusion, forward-looking remark: The future workplace should focus on human-machine collaboration rather than total robotic substitution.

## advantages — id `advantages`
Prompt: In many countries, an increasing number of people choose to work from home. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?
Order rule: Body 1 = the side you consider WEAKER (the disadvantages, acknowledged and bounded); Body 2 = the side you consider STRONGER (the advantages, expanded and emphasised). End on your strongest argument.
1. Intro, paraphrase: The shift toward remote work and telecommuting has accelerated rapidly across numerous modern economies.
2. Intro, position on balance: While remote employment can foster social isolation and weaken team cohesion, I argue that the gains in work-life autonomy and reduced commuting stress clearly outweigh these drawbacks.
3. Body 1, topic sentence: On the one hand, telecommuting introduces noticeable psychological and organisational challenges.
4. Body 1, explanation: The blurring of domestic and professional boundaries often leads to extended workdays and chronic feelings of professional disconnection.
5. Body 1, example: Studies among remote software engineers demonstrate that individuals operating in isolation frequently report elevated feelings of burnout due to diminished social interactions.
6. Body 1, concession/transition: These communicative frictions undeniably represent a significant obstacle for distributed organisations.
7. Body 2, topic sentence: On the other hand, the benefits of working from home in terms of flexibility and productivity are far more substantial.
8. Body 2, explanation 1: Eliminating long daily commutes saves employees substantial time and energy, which can be redirected into physical exercise, family commitments, or focused work.
9. Body 2, explanation 2/example: Furthermore, remote work enables staff to craft personalised environments free from common office interruptions, which empirical surveys link to sharper task completion rates.
10. Body 2, direct comparative anchor: The measurable enhancements to daily well-being and sustained focus substantially eclipse the solvable issues of workplace isolation.
11. Conclusion, direct evaluation: In conclusion, although working from home can generate feelings of solitude, its capacity to grant temporal autonomy and reduce commuting strain makes its advantages far more compelling.
12. Conclusion, recommendation: Organisations that adopt flexible hybrid frameworks will likely capture these benefits while mitigating the downsides.

## problem-solution — id `problem-solution`
Prompt: Many major cities suffer from severe traffic congestion. What are the primary causes of this issue, and what measures can be taken to solve it?
Order rule: Body 1 = the causes/problems; Body 2 = the solutions. (Not problem+solution pairs.)
1. Intro, paraphrase the problem: Urban vehicular gridlock has emerged as one of the most pressing infrastructure dilemmas confronting metropolitan centres worldwide.
2. Intro, roadmap thesis: This phenomenon is driven primarily by inadequate public transit and rapid urban sprawl, but it can be effectively mitigated through congestion pricing and heavy state investment in rail infrastructure.
3. Body 1, topic sentence: The root cause of excessive traffic congestion is the widespread dependency on private vehicles coupled with deficient transit networks.
4. Body 1, explanation: When bus and commuter train networks are unreliable, crowded, or poorly integrated, commuters naturally prefer the comfort and direct routing of personal cars.
5. Body 1, supporting factor/example: For instance, rapid suburban expansion in metropolitan hubs often outpaces railway development, forcing suburban residents onto already saturated arterial highways.
6. Body 1, consequence anchor: This infrastructural shortfall creates bottlenecks that persist well beyond standard rush hours.
7. Body 2, topic sentence: To resolve this crisis, municipal authorities must implement a combination of regulatory disincentives and infrastructure upgrades.
8. Body 2, solution 1 and mechanism: First, municipal governments should levy congestion charges on private vehicles entering central business districts, thereby reducing non-essential inner-city traffic.
9. Body 2, solution 2 and mechanism: Concurrently, tax revenues derived from these levies should be channelled into high-capacity light-rail networks, ensuring that commuters have rapid, cost-effective alternatives.
10. Body 2, projected impact: If cities execute this dual approach, traffic density can be reduced quickly and sustainably.
11. Conclusion, synthesise causes and solutions: To summarise, while urban gridlock stems from vehicle dependency and underfunded public transit, it can be overcome by imposing vehicular tariffs and expanding reliable rail services.
12. Conclusion, call to action: Without decisive urban planning interventions, expanding populations will render city streets permanently choked.

## two-part — id `two-part`
Prompt: Happiness is considered very important in life. Why is it difficult to define? What factors are necessary to achieve it?
Order rule: Introduction answers BOTH questions directly in its thesis. Body 1 = question 1; Body 2 = question 2.
1. Intro, paraphrase: The concept of happiness is widely regarded as the ultimate human objective, yet its precise nature remains elusive.
2. Intro, two-part thesis: Happiness is inherently difficult to define because it is subjective and changes throughout life, and achieving it depends chiefly on meaningful human connections and strong physical health.
3. Body 1, topic sentence: The primary reason happiness resists objective definition is that its criteria differ drastically from one individual to another.
4. Body 1, explanation: Because personal fulfilment is anchored in individual values, what brings euphoria to an ambitious entrepreneur might trigger exhaustion in an artist or scholar.
5. Body 1, temporal shift/contrast: Additionally, personal benchmarks evolve over time; a young adult may prioritise career progression, whereas an elderly individual often equates happiness with physical autonomy and tranquillity.
6. Body 1, anchor for question 1: This inherent variability prevents any single psychological metric from universally capturing the state.
7. Body 2, topic sentence: Nevertheless, certain foundational elements are universally required to foster sustained well-being.
8. Body 2, factor 1 (health and security): Foremost among these is baseline physical and psychological health, without which enjoying everyday accomplishments becomes exceedingly difficult.
9. Body 2, factor 2 (social connection, with evidence): Equally vital is the presence of supportive interpersonal relationships, as extensive sociological studies demonstrate that loneliness is the single greatest predictor of chronic distress.
10. Body 2, anchor for question 2: Consequently, robust health combined with deep community bonds forms the true bedrock of contentment.
11. Conclusion, restate answers to both questions: In conclusion, while the fluid, deeply subjective nature of human values makes happiness difficult to standardise, it universally demands robust personal health and caring human relationships.
12. Conclusion, final thought: Rather than chasing abstract ideals of perfection, individuals should cultivate these two tangible pillars in daily life.

## Quick reference

| Type | Thesis pattern | Body 1 | Body 2 | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion | Direct stance + 2 reasons | Main reason 1 | Main reason 2 | Restate stance, synthesise |
| Discussion | Paraphrase both sides + own stance | View 1 (objective) | View 2 + own stance | Summarise both views, confirm verdict |
| Advantages vs disadvantages | State whether pros or cons outweigh | Lesser side (bounded) | Dominant side (expanded) | Reassert stronger side |
| Problem and solution | Central cause + path to solution | 1-2 root causes | 1-2 concrete interventions | Summarise causes and paired solutions |
| Direct questions | Direct answers to Q1 and Q2 | Developed answer to Q1 | Developed answer to Q2 | Synthesise both answers |
